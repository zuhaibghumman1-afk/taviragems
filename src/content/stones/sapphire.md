## Overview

Sapphire is the gem variety of the mineral corundum, an aluminum oxide. Pure corundum is colorless; trace amounts of iron and titanium create the classic blue, while other elements produce pink, yellow, green and purple stones. Red corundum is the one exception: it is called ruby.

With a hardness of 9 on the Mohs scale, sapphire is second only to diamond among natural gems. That durability makes it one of the best choices for rings worn every day.

## Origins

The most celebrated blue sapphires came from Kashmir, where a small deposit discovered in the 1880s produced a soft, velvety blue that is still the benchmark for the color. Today, most fine sapphires come from Sri Lanka, Madagascar and Myanmar.
